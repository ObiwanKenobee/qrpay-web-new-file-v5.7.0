## 1. Objective

Identify one accountable Nakuru-area organization that could operate a small, non-cash community capability-coordination pilot with Sanctum Scan / Atlas Care.

The pilot must start from a real service already delivered or intended by the operator, not from a software feature looking for a problem. The partner retains responsibility for its service; the platform may support scoped coordination, commitments, evidence classification and review.

### Material due-diligence gaps

1. **Registration evidence:** MAL's transparency page describes the certificate, constitution and strategic plan PDFs as interim reference PDFs and says official stamped scans are to replace them when ready. Obtain the current certified registration evidence and verify it with the issuing authority. Confirm the precise legal form and current status; do not infer PBO status from a website description.
2. **Safeguarding:** The public safeguarding page states a fuller safeguarding policy document will be uploaded. Obtain and review the complete policy, named safeguarding lead, referral/escalation procedure, complaint channel, staff training evidence and available response capacity before any pilot involving participants.
3. **Privacy and data governance:** Obtain the current privacy/data-protection policy, data-flow map, retention schedule, rights request process, incident procedure and any relevant controller/processor agreements. A website policy page alone is not a pilot-specific legal review.
4. **Financial and programme stewardship:** Request recent audited accounts, annual reports, project budgets and a sample of programme monitoring reports. Verify the source and period of any impact claim.
5. **Actual service capacity:** Confirm which programme is currently active, its operating location, named service owner, group(s) served, staff/volunteer capacity, seasonality, resource availability and what happens when demand exceeds capacity.
6. **Authority and conflicts:** Confirm governing-body authority to enter an agreement, identify the authorized representative, document conflicts of interest and obtain references from existing partners.
7. **Participant agency and accessibility:** Confirm how the community group co-designs the work, how participation is voluntary, how participants can challenge/correct records, and which accessibility arrangements are practical without collecting unnecessary sensitive details.

**Stop condition:** Until items 1–3 are satisfactorily resolved by accountable reviewers, MAL remains a discovery lead only. Do not create or collect live cases, names, contacts, disability/health details, photographs or individual outcome records.

## 3. Proposed pilot hypothesis — subject to MAL and community co-design

### Working title

**One community group, one climate-smart livelihoods intervention, one evidence trail.**

### Proposed service boundary

Explore whether MAL would be willing and able to operate one small adult community-group activity within an existing Nakuru livelihoods or climate-resilience programme. A possible starting point is a climate-smart kitchen-garden or community nursery learning activity, but only if MAL confirms it is current, needed by the group and adequately resourced.

The hypothesis is that a small amount of transparent coordination could help an operator match an agreed group-level need with non-cash capabilities—such as a trainer, locally appropriate seedlings, compost, tools, demonstration materials or transport—and then record whether each accepted commitment was delivered.

This is a conversation hypothesis, not an agreed project, budget or commitment.

### Proposed scope limits

- One adult community/farmer group chosen by the operator and community, not selected by an automated system.
- One small activity at one existing programme site.
- One non-cash service or in-kind capability coordination workflow.
- One accountable operator and named human coordinator.
- No financial collection, donation custody, disbursement, lending, wallet or payment integration.
- No public beneficiary QR, case search or individual profile.
- No minors or safeguarding-sensitive case management in the initial digital workflow.
- No person-level identity, contact, health, disability, household location or case narrative in the software discovery exercise.
- Record only group-level information and aggregate activity measures if the operator, community and qualified reviewers approve that design.
- No offline consequential action or synchronization.

### Candidate workflow to validate

1. Community group and operator describe a bounded group-level need and desired outcome.
2. Operator confirms the need, scope, capacity and delivery owner.
3. Potential non-cash capabilities are declared with quantity, unit, availability window and constraints.
4. System presents a transparent feasibility proposal; a named human approves, rejects or modifies it with a reason.
5. Each contributor accepts a specific commitment and due window.
6. Delivery is declared by an attributable actor and supported by proportionate evidence.
7. A distinct reviewer checks delivery where required by the pilot.
8. Operator and community review a baseline and follow-up observation, with limitations recorded.

A need is not an entitlement; a match is not a commitment; a commitment is not delivery; delivery is not an outcome; an observed change is not proof of causation.

### Candidate measures

Only if the operator and community agree:
- Session/activity delivered against plan.
- Number of adult participants, reported only as an aggregate.
- In-kind commitments accepted and fulfilled within the agreed window.
- Demonstration plot or nursery established and maintained at follow-up.
- Agreed practice-adoption observation at 30/60/90 days.
- Participant feedback and challenges, captured without unnecessary identity data.

Do not claim yield improvement, income growth, climate resilience or ecological restoration from a single workshop or self-reported attendance. Any such outcome needs an appropriate baseline, denominator, follow-up period, method and reviewer.

## 5. Legal and registration verification route

Kenya's Public Benefit Organizations Regulatory Authority (PBORA) states that the PBO Act was operationalized effective 14 May 2024 and that PBORA is responsible for registration and regulation of charitable organizations in Kenya. This makes it important to verify the exact legal form, current registration status and applicable authority for each prospective organization rather than relying on an old NGO certificate or website claim.

Official starting point:
- https://www.pbora.go.ke/pbora/overview
- https://pbora.go.ke/

Where the candidate is registered as a CBO with a county department rather than as a PBO, verify that exact status with the issuing county office and obtain qualified advice on the applicable requirements for the proposed activity and data processing. This document makes no legal determination.

## 6. First engagement agenda — do not send without approval

A 30–45 minute exploratory conversation should establish:

1. Which current adult community-group activity has a real coordination problem that the operator wants to solve?
2. Who owns delivery and what capacity is committed today?
3. Which resources or skills are genuinely scarce, and which non-cash contributors could supply them?
4. What records are already kept, by whom, and what evidence supports delivery?
5. What information can be kept at group level, without collecting participant identities or sensitive details?
6. Who is accountable for safeguarding, complaints, privacy, security and service delivery?
7. What baseline and follow-up outcome can be measured without overstating impact?
8. What would make the organization say no to a digital pilot?

A no is a valid outcome. Do not offer funding, software deployment, public promotion, data hosting or impact claims without separate authority and agreement.

## 7. Qualification decision

**Current recommended lead to approach:** Move for Adaptive Locals (MAL), through its published partnership channel.

**Why:** It is visibly Nakuru-based, publishes relevant community livelihood, agroecology, climate-resilience and community-capacity programmes, and has an explicit partnership process. Its public materials also expose clear due-diligence gaps rather than concealing them; those gaps must be resolved before pilot approval.

**Recommended referral route:** NETI may be asked to identify or validate additional current member organizations operating in farmer/community livelihoods.

**Technical alternative:** NECOFA may be considered for agroecology expertise after legal identity, current status and contact inconsistencies are resolved.

**Decision today:** No partner selected, contacted or approved. No pilot charter signed. No live participant data collected.

## 8. Required evidence before a pilot can be marked qualified

- [ ] Current registration/legal-status evidence checked with the correct issuing authority.
- [ ] Governing body and authorized representative verified.
- [ ] Current programme/site and delivery capacity confirmed directly.
- [ ] Recent partner references and conflicts of interest reviewed.
- [ ] Full safeguarding policy, named lead and practical escalation capacity reviewed.
- [ ] Privacy/data-flow/retention/rights process reviewed for the exact pilot.
- [ ] Participant co-design, voluntary participation, accessibility and challenge process accepted.
- [ ] Group-level baseline and outcome method agreed.
- [ ] In-kind capability sources and commitments are genuine and accepted.
- [ ] Accountable operator and maintainers record a written qualification decision.

**M029-A1 exit condition:** A named organization has completed the checks above, agreed to a bounded pilot in writing, and identified accountable operating, privacy, safeguarding and measurement owners. Until then, the lead remains a lead — not a partner.
